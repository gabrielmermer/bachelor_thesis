Large group of participants described the experience as a deep, immersive and absorbing experience. What stands out is that universal feeling of excitement. Each participant felt excited for using the voice input system, but that emotion doesn't have to do with the input system itself but rather the fact that it was s novel experience for all of the present participant.

On the other hand some participants reported a higher feeling of control and immersion as a direct result of open-endedness that voice input allowed.
This feeling of higher immersion was not universal, leaving a portion of participants less immersed when using voice input due to beforementioned feeling lack of control or other factors that broke their immersion (such as processing delay or the externalisation of their thoughts via voice).

The implications of above dynamic indicate that participants not only felt differently when exposed to those two input modalities, but also clearly indicated that some emotions like comfort can be more crucial to them when making a choice between voice or keyboard versions.

\section{Limitations}

It should be noted that the whole process was at least a little bit limited by the Observer Effect. The sole fact that the participants knew that they were being observed had an impact on the results of the observations. This was especially evident when participants used their voice to navigate, since despite any efforts from the author's side the act of speaking to the computer culturally feels more intimate compared to using keyboard. This is not only pure speculation but was directly mentioned by one of the participants who implied that her emotions during playing would've been different had she been not under observation. 

Also, as discussed before the novelty effect of being exposed to a new form of input had an emotional impact on participants, making them excited to try the voice input and altering their perception of it due to this being their first experience with voice-driven navigation in games. This effect is hard to filter out completely but the fact that participants had a clear preference split between the input version suggests that it's effects were not overwhelming, rather turning it into something to keep in mind.



\section{Future research}

Future research about this topic could include trying to find the cause for the obsreverd variance in emotional impact of friction experienced in-game. Same goes towards the feelings of immersion - the lack of a clear model that explained why some particiapnts felt more immersed than others is a question waiting to be answered.
