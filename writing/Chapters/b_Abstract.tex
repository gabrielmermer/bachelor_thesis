This thesis explores the emotional impact of using voice to control a video game.
It seeks to answer how it feels to use voice to navigate and how different it is from using a keyboard to control the game.
To answer these questions, a game was developed that incorporated natural language processing powered by an LLM to allow for understanding natural language commands. The game could be controlled either by voice or by keyboard.
10 participants tested the game and were interviewed afterwards. 
Findings suggest that frustration and immersion were the most prominent emotions that emerged when using voice.
Players often reported feeling more immersed while also feeling less comfortable compared to the keyboard version.
Emotional impact varied between players, showcasing the need for further studies to determine the cause of this split.
Most of the time, using voice made players feel less comfortable compared to a keyboard, mostly due to higher friction and increased cognitive load.
The feeling of immersion varied between players. Some found using voice to be more immersive, while others reported weakened immersion. 
Findings suggest that features that influence player immersion vary between players.
Comfort turned out to be the most important emotional difference between the versions, as players often preferred the version they felt more comfortable in.
The reasons for differences in the emotional impact between participants are something that would benefit from further research.
