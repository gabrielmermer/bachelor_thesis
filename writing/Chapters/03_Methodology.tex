\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:method}


This chapter addresses the user testing. It delves into the test design, the process for analysing the results and the participants' information. 

\section{Testing sample}

The sample size for this study was 10 participants divided equally between genders. 
All the participants were Polish, with English being their secondary language. Players' English fluency hovered between B2 and C1.
Eight out of ten of the users were in their early to mid twenties, with two of them in their forties.
The vast majority of the players had higher education, and all of them had some experience with voice assistants, none of which were positive.
Each of them had some prior experience in gaming, ranging from childhood memories to being semi-professional gamers. Two of the participants had a game development background. None of the players had experience with VUI in games.


\section{User testing design}

Given that emotions are the core of the research questions, the study used qualitative methods.
The design for the experiment was a within-subjects A/B test between the text and audio versions of the game, combined with an interview afterwards.

Firstly, participants played the audio version for 10 minutes. After their time was up, the researcher switched the game to a text version and let the players continue for an additional 10 minutes.
There was no counterbalancing in this testing sequence due to the observed experience carry-over for the first three participants.
Those three participants played the keyboard version first. 
Seeing the keyboard version UI allowed them to see the boundaries of the game, and it shaped the language that they chose when interacting with the game using their voice.
To reduce this effect, the testing order was reversed, at the cost of no counterbalancing.

                                                           
As a next step, participants were interviewed following a pre-defined script.
The questions asked during the interview were designed to make the participants talk about the emotions that they felt during the experience and to contrast how they felt between the two game versions. On top of pre-defined questions, participants were asked follow-up questions. Those probing questions were a result of what the participants mentioned or demonstrated, and they were used to further understand participants' feelings.





\section{Interpreting results}


Each of the interviews was later analysed following the thematic analysis framework suggested by \textcite{mannResearchInterview2016}.
This process was used to identify 4 themes per participant. 
After that work, all the themes were clustered together, following the principles of \textcite{chuntieGroundedTheoryResearch2019}. This resulted in five main categories emerging.

\section{Objectivity check}

For all of the tests, the author made sure to remind himself to be as objective as possible and not to defend his creation.
After each session, he assessed if he wasn't defensive about the game itself.
Ideally, the person who's conducting the research should be different from the game's developer, but self-reflective behaviour helped to maintain an objective perspective.
