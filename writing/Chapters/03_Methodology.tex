\section{Testing sample}

The sample size for this study was 10 participants. This number was chosen as a compromise between the desired quality of work and available time.
All the participants were Polish, with English being their secondary language. Players' English fluency hovered between B2 and C1.
Most of the users were in their early to mid twenties, a few of them in their forties.
The vast majority of the players had higher education, and all of them had some experience with voice assistants, none of which were positive.
Each of them had some prior experience in gaming, ranging from childhood memories to being semi-professional gamers. Two of the participants had a game development background.


\section{User testing design}

Given the time constraints, the study used qualitative methods.
The design for the experiment was an A/B test between the text and audio versions of the game, combined with an interview afterwards.
Firstly, participants played the audio version for 10 minutes. After their time was up, the researcher switched the game to a text version and let the players continue for an additional 10 minutes. As a next step, participants were interviewed following a pre-defined script.
The questions asked during the interview were designed to make the participants talk about the emotions that they felt during the experience and to contrast how they felt between the two game versions. On top of pre-defined questions, participants were asked follow-up questions. Those probing questions were a result of what the participants mentioned or demonstrated, and they were used to further understand participants' feelings.


or the first three participants, the order of the testing was reversed. They first played the keyboard version and then the audio one. However, it was noted that this order of the A/B influenced the participants by showing them the boundaries of the game. Additionally, they used the same language when speaking to the game as the text version had used in the UI. This led to the reversal of the testing order for those two versions and resulted in lowering of this carry-over effect.

\section{Objectivity check}

For all of the tests, the researcher made sure to remind himself to be as objective as possible and not to defend his creation.
After each session, he assessed if he wasn't defensive about the game itself.
Ideally, the person who's conducting the research should be different from the game's author, but the self-reflective behaviour helped to maintain an objective perspective.
