Below are the aggregated findings, meta-themes. Things that came to me only after analysing all of the interviews.


\section{A - Modality transfer}

Players seem to be transfering their knowledge from keyboard navigation games into the voice driven experience. This is very exemplified when they played the voice version right after trying the baseline version. ... 

The first three participants of the testing had the baseline keyboard version exposed to them first. This led to them transfering their command patterns about the game into the voice-driven version. 
To lessen the effect of that transfer for the 7 tests that followed the order got flipped - the participants were first presented with the voice version of the game. This reduced this issue, but didn't fully eliminate it.

People still used their pre-existing notions about voice control and their experience with other input modalities transfered into this experience. This can't be fully eliminated but the fact that they didn't play the keyboard verions had a noticable impact on the players interaction with the game.

\section{C - Friction vectors and impact on the experience}

The experience had a lot of pain points for all participants. What's interesting is how same errors were more or less severe for different participants. Some errors were very painful for some while others treated them as an additional part of the experience or even a feature. 
This is very interesting since it shows how differently people interepret errors.

\section{D - Impact of Agency and affordance perception on player feelings}

The voice-driven experience doesn't show it's boundaries. They have to be discovered by the players. This ambiguity made some players anxious while others rejoyced. The players also probed the system for affordances, trying to see what's possible.


\section{E - The relationship between command language and the game experience}

Players all adopted their own language when talking with the computer and nearly always it stemed from their existing relationship with either computers, technology, voice assistants or some other notions that come from speakng to someone for permision. The language that they chose was either commanding or cooperative. This in turn shaped their emotions while playing the game. This type of unpredictable behaviour is absent in the keyboard driven experience. 

\section{F - Accessibility context}

Some players mentioned how this could be a good experience for people who can't use keyboard navigation.
